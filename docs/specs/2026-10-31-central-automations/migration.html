Moving to central

The first time a machine runs the new release, it hands its schedules, live wakes and their instructions to central, once. IDs, targets, creators and settings are kept, and timing is fitted to your new rules. At every moment exactly one side owns each automation's clock, so nothing fires twice and nothing is skipped.

Converting timing to the new rules

Each definition is converted once into a timing the new rules accept, so nothing is ever fitted again after the move. The examples come from your own data.

The handover reports how many were adjusted or ended, so you can see it.

Why it's safe

  • One owner, no waiting: the machine stops admitting before anything is sent. A run that's still going, or whose outcome is uncertain, keeps holding its schedule. Central starts nothing for that schedule until the machine reports the run truly finished.
  • No double fire from fitting: central compares original due times with the machine's timer mark. A one-shot the machine fired at 10:02 can't fire again at 10:05.
  • Crash at any step: it resumes with the same frozen bytes, and central answers a repeat with alreadyImported.
  • Old releases: a machine still on the old release never imports. It keeps firing its own schedules until it upgrades, and central never fires them.
