How it all fits

Three places, three jobs.

  • Central's Fleet object holds the one list of schedules and wakes, and the clock.
  • Central's per-machine notice journal tells each machine a batch is ready, and holds that notice until the machine catches up.
  • Each machine's Router takes its firings and pushes them into its sessions, the same way any Router message arrives today.

Nothing polls. Central sleeps until the next due time, and machines sleep on an open socket until central sends a hint.

The picture

A worked example: 3 machines, 24 automations

You have 9 schedules and 15 wakes spread over sessions on machines A, B and C. Everything sits on the 5-minute grid. At the 10:00 mark, five are due: two wakes for sessions on A, two schedule runs on B, and one wake for a session on C. C's laptop lid is closed.

One alarm wakes the Fleet object

There's a single alarm, set to the earliest due time among all 24. In one transaction it records all five firings, advances each automation's next due time, sets the alarm to the next earliest (say 10:05), and goes back to sleep. One wake-up, not five.

Three batches, three "ready" notices

One message per machine: A's batch holds its two wakes, B's its two runs, and C's its one wake. That commit is the cutoff, so a pause after it gets the answer alreadySent. Each machine gets a small "batch ready" notice through its notice journal, as a hint down its open socket, and fetches the batch. C has no socket open, so its notice waits in the journal.

A and B pull their items and push them into sessions

For each entry, the Router writes a receipt and the queued work in one transaction, so a crash can't lose it. A's Router delivers two ⏰ Router wake pushes. The Codex session is idle, so a turn starts. The Claude session is mid-turn, so the wake is queued and prompted once that turn ends. B's Router starts two scheduled runs in their bound sessions. Each machine records a receipt per firing, so a re-sent copy is ignored.

Outcomes flow back

A and B report "delivered", "run started" and later "run finished" with the summary. schedule show from any of the three machines shows the same thing.

C wakes up

C reconnects, catches up its notice journal and fetches its batch. Each entry is judged on its own. A wake whose end has passed is expired, and you can see that from A or B. A schedule entry has no expiry and runs now.

How a wake reaches a session

This part is today's code, unchanged. A firing becomes the same ⏰ Router wake push as now. The Router picks one route for the target session (Codex app-server, Claude Code peer, or an ACP provider such as Cursor) and keeps it fixed for that attempt.
