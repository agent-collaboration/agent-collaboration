How it all fits

Three places, three jobs.

  • Central's Fleet object holds the one list of schedules and wakes, and the clock.
  • Central's mailboxes hold each firing until its machine takes it.
  • Each machine's Router takes its firings and pushes them into its sessions, the same way any Router message arrives today.

Nothing polls. Central sleeps until the next due time, and machines sleep on an open socket until central sends a hint.

The picture

A worked example: 3 machines, 24 automations

You have 9 schedules and 15 wakes spread over sessions on machines A, B and C. At 10:00, five are due: two wakes for sessions on A, two schedule runs on B, one wake for a session on C. C's laptop lid is closed.

One alarm wakes the Fleet object

There's a single alarm, set to the earliest due time among all 24. In one transaction it records all five firings, advances each automation's next due time, sets the alarm to the next earliest (say 10:05), and goes back to sleep. One wake-up, not five.

Five messages go into three mailboxes

Two go into A's, two into B's, and one into C's. Each mailbox's connection object sends a small "you have mail" hint down that machine's open socket. C has no socket open, so its item just waits.

A and B pull their items and push them into sessions

A's Router delivers two ⏰ Router wake pushes. The Codex session is idle, so a turn starts. The Claude session is mid-turn, so the wake is queued and prompted once that turn ends. B's Router starts two scheduled runs in their bound sessions. Each machine records a receipt per firing, so a re-sent copy is ignored.

Outcomes flow back

A and B report "delivered", "run started" and later "run finished" with the summary. schedule show from any of the three machines shows the same thing.

C wakes up

C reconnects and catches up from its mailbox position. If the wake's end time hasn't passed, it's delivered now, once. If it has, central records it as expired, and you can see that from A or B.

How a wake reaches a session

This part is today's code, unchanged. A firing becomes the same ⏰ Router wake push as now. The Router picks one route for the target session (Codex app-server, Claude Code peer, or an ACP provider such as Cursor) and keeps it fixed for that attempt.

Why dozens, or hundreds, stay cheap

ThingHow manyEffect
Alarmsone for the whole Fleet objectset to the earliest due time; everything due together is handled in one wake-up
Socketsone per machinesleep when idle, which costs no billed duration; a hint wakes them
Active wakesat most one per sessionagents can't flood a session
Schedule runsat most one running and one waiting per scheduleextra due times fold in; no backlog burst after an outage
Undelivered wakeabsorbs new due timesa sleeping machine wakes to one wake, not a pile

All automations sit in one Fleet object. Tens or hundreds are fine. If you ever have thousands with second-level timing, the fix is to round due times to the minute, so each minute is one wake-up. That isn't needed now.
