Where an approval goes

Example. Your Lead starts an Implementer. The Implementer tries to write outside its project. The request goes to the Lead and to any client watching the Implementer (a Codex CLI, the CLI). The Lead says yes: the write happens and everyone else is told "decided by the Lead". If the Lead can't decide, it forwards the request to central, and you see it in the CLI (and later in your UI).

Central decides. The approval itself lives at central, and central counts the first answer, wherever it comes from; anyone answering later is told who already decided. The Mac only holds the agent's waiting request and applies central's answer. A client attached to the session answers through the Mac, and the answer goes on to central.

Central holds the approval record and decides first-answer-wins; the Mac holds only the waiting request; a directly attached client is a destination whose answer goes to central. While central is unreachable, approvals wait.

Depends on it: R8, R9, R9a.

What "auto" means

Example. A Claude delegate started with no mode edits files in its project and writes to its own scratch folder (<router root>/scratch/session-<id>/) without asking. When it wants to write in your home folder, that becomes an approval, routed as above. It is never just refused.

Sending to a running agent

Example. A Cursor agent is mid-task and you send "also update the README". Cursor can't take a message mid-turn, so the Router stops the turn and starts a new one with your message first; you get back restarted. A Claude agent takes it mid-turn; you get steered.
